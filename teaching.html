Overview

How do frontier AI systems acquire their capabilities, and how can we study them scientifically?

This advanced seminar examines frontier AI across the full pipeline: training data, learning objectives, scaling laws, synthetic data, post-training, and emerging agentic systems. We will focus on open questions about how training shapes model behavior and what increasingly capable systems may be able to do—including scientific discovery, continual learning, adaptation, and self-improvement.

We will also study how to evaluate and govern these capabilities responsibly, including questions around model evaluation, scalable oversight, and detecting concerning behaviors before deployment.

The course combines lectures, interactive role-playing paper seminars, and a final research project. It is intended for PhD students interested in foundation models, scaling, agents, reasoning, evaluation, and AI safety.

Format

This course combines lectures with paper presentations by students, encouraging both fundamental knowledge acquisition and open-ended discussion of new research directions. Paper discussions will involve role-playing student seminars.

Prerequisites

There are no official prerequisites, but a comfortable grasp of probability, linear algebra, and machine learning is expected. This is an advanced, research-focused seminar.

Course Requirements

  • Paper presentation.
  • Final research project.
